How to Send a Large PDF by Email

By Zainab Fuzail · Updated November 2026

You attach a PDF and your email service says it is too large, or the receiver’s inbox rejects it. Most limits can be solved without any special software. This guide shows three ways to shrink what you send, in the order I recommend trying them.

Why Does Email Reject Large Files?

Email services set a limit on the size of attachments, often around 20 to 25 MB, and the receiver’s service may have its own limit. Several attachments in one message are added together, so two files that are fine alone can be too large as a pair. A scanned document or a file full of photos reaches the limit much faster than typed text. Always check the exact limit of your email service, because it can change.

Option 1: Compress the PDF

Use Compress PDF with the Recommended level. This works very well for scans and photo-heavy files. In my test, a 6-page practice PDF of 5,910.2 KB became 1,344 KB with the Recommended level, about 77% smaller. The pages become images, so the text can no longer be selected. Keep your original. More details are in our guide on meeting an upload size limit.

Option 2: Send Only the Pages That Are Needed

If the receiver only needs a few pages, extract them with Split PDF. Pages are copied as they are, so the quality does not change, and a file with fewer pages is always smaller than the whole document. Our guide on extracting a single page explains how to type the page numbers.

Option 3: Send the PDF in Parts

For a long file, cut it into parts and send each part in a separate email. Follow our guide on splitting a PDF into parts, and tell the receiver the order of the parts. Name the files with numbers, such as part-1 and part-2, so nothing gets mixed up.

A Simple Example

Suppose you must email a 30 MB scanned file to an office whose inbox accepts only 10 MB. First compress it with the Recommended level and check the new size. If it is now under 10 MB, attach it and send. If it is still too large, extract only the pages the office asks for, or send the file in two or three parts and write the order in your message.

Check Before You Send

  • Open the new file and scroll through every page.
  • Zoom in on small text and signatures.
  • Check the size in your file manager against the email limit.
  • Keep the original file.

Which Option Should You Choose?

  • A scan or photo-based file: compress it.
  • A long document where only some pages matter: extract those pages.
  • A long document that must be sent whole: send it in parts, or compress it first.

Tips for a Smoother Email

  • Write a clear subject line and name the file, for example “Application - Your Name.pdf”.
  • If you send several parts, say how many there are, such as “part 1 of 3”.
  • Combine related documents into one file with Merge PDF before compressing, so the receiver opens only one attachment. On a phone, see our guide on merging PDF files on Android and iPhone.
  • For job applications, see our guide on preparing a job application PDF.
  • If your pages are photos, read how to turn photos of notes into one PDF before you compress.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Compressing the same file again and again. Quality drops each time. Always start from the original.
  • Sending without opening the new file. Check every page first.
  • Forgetting that attachments add up. Check the total size of the email, not only one file.
  • Deleting the original. Keep it until the receiver confirms they got the right file.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the easiest way to make a PDF smaller for email?

For a scan or a photo-based file, use Compress PDF with the Recommended level. For a long document, send only the pages that are needed.

Will compressing make the text unreadable?

The text stays readable on screen, but it becomes part of an image, so it can no longer be selected or searched. Zoom in to check small print before you send.

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. PaperDesk works inside your browser. See our Privacy Policy.

Can I send a PDF in parts?

Yes. Cut it into page ranges, send each part in its own email, and tell the receiver the order.
