How to Save PDF Pages as PNG Images

By Zainab Fuzail · Updated November 2026

Sometimes you need a page of a PDF as a picture: to post in a chat, to place on a slide, or to show on a web page. This guide explains how to turn the pages of a PDF into PNG images in your browser and how to keep the downloads tidy.

When Do You Need a Page as an Image?

  • Chats and social media: share one page as a picture that opens instantly, without a PDF viewer.
  • Presentations: place a page of a report on a slide.
  • Websites and blogs: show a form or certificate as an image on a page.
  • Forms that accept only images: some portals take JPG or PNG but not PDF.

Before You Start

  • Open the PDF and note which pages you need, so you download only those.
  • Remove any password from the PDF first.
  • Create a folder for the images, because every page downloads as a separate file.
  • If the PDF is very long, take out the pages you need first with Split PDF.

Step 1: Convert the PDF

  1. Open PDF to Image.
  2. Click “Choose PDF File” and select your document.
  3. Click “Convert to Images” and wait while the pages are processed.
  4. A list of pages appears. Click “Download PNG” next to each page you want.

Each page is saved as its own image, named paperdesk-page-1.png, paperdesk-page-2.png and so on.

A Simple Example

Imagine a 10-page report and you want to show only the chart on page 4 in a presentation. You open the tool, convert the PDF, and click “Download PNG” next to page 4 only. You get one image file that you can insert straight into a slide, without keeping the other nine pages.

Download Only What You Need

You do not have to download every page. If you need one page, click only that button. For a long document, use Split PDF first so the converter works on a smaller file. Our guide on extracting a single page explains how to type the page numbers.

About Quality and Size

Pages are created at a high resolution, so text and pictures stay clear. The files can be fairly large, especially for pages full of photos. PNG keeps sharp edges well, which suits documents.

PNG or PDF: Which One to Send?

  • Send a PNG when the receiver only needs to look at one page, for example in a chat or on a slide.
  • Send a PDF when there are several pages, or when the document will be printed.
  • Large images: if a PNG is too big to send, read how to send large files by email, or put the image into a PDF and use Compress PDF.

Turning Images Back Into a PDF

Use Image to PDF. It accepts JPG and PNG files and puts each image on its own A4 page. Our guide on turning photos of notes into one PDF explains the steps. To join several of those PDFs, use Merge PDF.

Tips for Tidy Downloads

  • Download only the pages you need, one by one.
  • Rename each image right away, for example “report-page-4.png”, so you can find it later.
  • Keep the images in one folder together with the original PDF.
  • Check each image by opening it before you share it.
  • Do not share pages that contain private details nobody asked for.

Common Problems

  • The PDF will not convert. It may be damaged or password-protected. Open it in a PDF viewer first.
  • The page feels slow. Your device does the work. Close other tabs and try fewer pages.
  • Too many downloads. Each page is a separate file. Download only the pages you need.
  • The image is too large to send. Photo-heavy pages create big PNG files. Use a PDF and compress it instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I save all pages in one click?

Each page has its own “Download PNG” button, so you click once for every page you want.

What is the file name of each image?

Images are named paperdesk-page-1.png, paperdesk-page-2.png and so on, following the page number.

Is the image quality good?

Pages are created at a high resolution, so text and pictures stay clear. The trade-off is that files can be fairly large.

Is my PDF uploaded to a server?

No. PaperDesk works inside your browser. See our Privacy Policy.
