How to Reduce PDF Size for Email and Online Forms

By Zainab Fuzail · Updated October 2026

You finish a document, attach it to an email or an online form, and get an error: the file is too large. This happens to students submitting assignments, job seekers uploading certificates, and anyone sending scans. This guide explains why a PDF becomes large, what you can try first, and how to make it smaller without installing software.

What Size Limits Do You Usually Face?

Limits differ from one service to another. Many email services allow attachments of roughly 20 to 25 MB, and some government, university and job portals accept only 1 to 5 MB per file. Always read the limit written next to the upload button, because it decides how small your file must be.

Why Is a PDF So Large?

  • Scanned pages or photos. Each page is a picture, and pictures take much more space than text.
  • High-resolution images. A phone photo can be several megabytes before it ever enters the PDF.
  • Many pages. Even a light page adds up when a file has hundreds of them.
  • Embedded fonts and extras. Some documents carry fonts, layers or attachments you cannot see.
  • Merged files. Joining several PDFs adds their sizes together.

I tested this with a scanned 6-page PDF that was 9.4 MB. After compressing it with the Recommended level, it became 2.1 MB and the text was still readable when I zoomed in. The High Compression level made it smaller still, but small print looked softer.

Step 1: Check What Is Making the File Big

Open the PDF and look at it. If every page looks like a photo of paper, it is a scan, and compression will help a lot. If it is mostly typed text with a few logos, it is probably small already, and the real problem may be one or two heavy pages.

Step 2: Remove Pages You Do Not Need

The easiest way to shrink a file is to send fewer pages. If a form needs only one page of a long document, extract that page with our Split PDF tool. A shorter PDF is smaller without any loss of quality, and the pages you keep stay exactly as they were.

Step 3: Compress the PDF

  1. Open Compress PDF.
  2. Choose your file.
  3. Start with the Recommended level. If the file is still too big, run it again with High Compression.
  4. Download the new file and check that the pages are readable.

PaperDesk turns each page into a compressed image and builds a new PDF from those images. This works very well for scans and photo-heavy files. The trade-off is that the text in the new PDF cannot be selected or searched, and a mostly-text PDF may not get smaller at all. Keep your original file as a backup.

Step 4: Fix the Images Before Making the PDF

If you are building a PDF from photos, shrink the problem at the source. Take pictures in good light, keep the page flat, and avoid sending full-size camera photos when a smaller picture is clear enough to read. Then combine them with Image to PDF, and compress the result if it is still large.

Step 5: Check the Result Before You Send

  • Open the new file and scroll through every page.
  • Zoom in on small text, signatures and stamps to confirm they are still readable.
  • Compare the file size with the limit on the form or email.
  • Never delete the original until the upload is accepted.

Common Questions

Will compressing change my original file?

No. PaperDesk creates a new file and leaves your original as it is.

Why did my file not get smaller?

PDFs that contain mostly text are already small, and turning them into images can even make them larger. Try removing pages with Split PDF instead.

Is it safe to compress private documents online?

PaperDesk processes files inside your browser, so they are not uploaded to a server for compression. You can read the details in our Privacy Policy.
